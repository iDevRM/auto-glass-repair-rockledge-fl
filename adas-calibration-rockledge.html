What Does ADAS Camera Calibration Involve?

ADAS calibration starts with a scan of the car’s computer to see which driver-assist systems are installed and whether any fault codes are stored. A static calibration sets printed targets at measured distances in front of the car while the camera learns them. A dynamic calibration has the car driven on marked roads at a set speed while the camera relearns lane lines. Many vehicles need one type, and some need both.

When Do You Need ADAS Camera Calibration?

After a windshield replacement

The camera mounts to the glass, so new glass changes its angle.

A dash warning after glass work

Lane or collision warning lights after an install mean the camera needs attention.

Lane-keep pulling the wrong way

A camera that is off by a small amount can steer you toward a lane line.

After an alignment or suspension work

Changes to the ride height or thrust angle can affect what the camera sees.

Why Does a New Windshield Throw Off the Camera?

The forward camera is mounted in a bracket bonded to the windshield, right behind the mirror. Every windshield sits a tiny bit differently in the frame, and even a change of a fraction of a degree moves where the camera is looking dozens of feet down the road. The car’s computer cannot correct for that on its own, so the camera has to be calibrated to the new glass position.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my car has ADAS?

Look for a camera housing behind the rearview mirror at the top of the windshield, or check whether your car has lane departure warning, lane-keep assist, automatic emergency braking or adaptive cruise control. If it has any of these, it most likely has a windshield camera that needs calibration after new glass.

Is ADAS calibration really required after a windshield replacement?

Most vehicle makers say yes. Their repair procedures call for calibration any time the windshield with a camera bracket is replaced. Skipping it can leave the camera aimed slightly wrong, which can make warnings late or cause lane-keep to steer the wrong way.

How long does ADAS calibration take?

Calibration usually takes 30 minutes to an hour, depending on the vehicle and the method. Some cars finish a dynamic calibration within a short drive, while others need a longer drive on clearly marked roads.

Will I get proof that the calibration was done?

Yes. You get a record showing the calibration was completed, which is useful for your insurance claim and for your own peace of mind. Keep it with your vehicle paperwork.
