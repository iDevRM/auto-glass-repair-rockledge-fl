What Does Door Glass Replacement Involve?

Door glass work starts with taking off the inside door panel and the water shield behind it. The technician vacuums broken glass out of the bottom of the door, then bolts the new window to the regulator, the track that moves it up and down. The window is run up and down to check its alignment in the seals, the panel goes back on and the cabin is vacuumed.

When Do You Need Door Glass Replacement?

A smashed side window

Break-ins and stray debris turn side glass into small cubes in one hit.

A window that fell into the door

Usually a failed regulator or a broken glass clip, not the glass itself.

A window that will not go up

A dead motor or regulator leaves the car open to rain and theft.

A cracked vent or quarter window

Small fixed glass pieces near the mirror or rear pillar.

Why Do Side Windows Fall Into the Door?

Most power windows ride on a regulator, a set of cables and pulleys or a scissor arm driven by a small motor. Heat and humidity dry out the plastic clips and pulleys, and a cable can fray and snap. When that happens the glass drops into the door or sits crooked in the track. Door glass in Florida also takes extra wear from being run up and down all day for drive-throughs and tolls.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a side window replacement take?

Most door glass replacements take about an hour, including vacuuming the glass out of the door and the cabin. A job that also needs a new regulator or motor can take a little longer. Unlike a windshield, there is no adhesive cure time, so the car is ready to drive right away.

Can you fix a window that fell down into the door?

Yes. A window that drops into the door usually has a broken regulator cable or a loose glass clip rather than broken glass. The technician opens the door panel, finds the failed part and replaces it so the window rides up and down straight again.

Is laminated side glass different from regular side glass?

Some newer cars use laminated side glass, which has a plastic layer like a windshield and cracks instead of shattering. It is quieter and harder to break into, and it needs the same type of glass as a replacement. We check your vehicle so the new window matches what came from the factory.

What should I do right after a break-in?

Take pictures for your insurance and police report, remove valuables and tape plastic over the opening to keep rain out. Avoid rolling the broken window up or down, because loose glass in the door can jam the regulator. Then call for a replacement.
